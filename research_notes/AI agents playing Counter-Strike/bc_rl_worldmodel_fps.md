# Learned policies for FPS / fast 3D games from pixels: BC, RL and world models (2024 – Nov 2026)

Scope note: this covers the non-LLM "learned policy" line (behavioral cloning, RL, world models) for pixels-in / keyboard-mouse-out agents in shooters and comparable fast 3D games. Older landmarks (FTW, ViZDoom, VPT, GT Sophy) are included only as context. "Peer-reviewed" = published at a venue (Nature, Science, NeurIPS, ICLR, CVPR, CoG, CGF); "self-reported" = arXiv preprint, blog, model card or GitHub README only. Where I could only read an abstract or a secondary summary, I say so.

Headline summary for the report writer:
- No published system plays Counter-Strike (CS:GO or CS2) from pixels at human level. The best peer-reviewed pixel-based CS agent (Pearce & Zhu, IEEE CoG 2022) reaches the level of the *medium built-in bot* in deathmatch and is far below good humans; nothing since 2022 has superseded it for CS specifically.
- The only pixel-based FPS result that is genuinely superhuman against human players remains DeepMind FTW (Quake III CTF, Science 2019) — a bespoke environment, 450k games of population-based RL, not transferable to a commercial game without an engine-level simulator.
- The 2025–2026 frontier for pixels-in/keyboard-mouse-out agents is large-scale behavioral cloning on thousands to tens of thousands of hours of gameplay video: Player2/Elefant AI's Pixels2Play (7k–8.3k h, 150M–1.2B params, open weights/data, 20 Hz on an RTX 5090) and NVIDIA's NitroGen (40k h, 1,000+ games, gamepad-only, CVPR 2026 oral, open weights). Both claim competent/"human-like" play in DOOM/Quake-era FPS, but neither reports win rates vs humans; Pixels2Play's "human-level" claim is self-reported and qualitative.
- World models: DIAMOND trained a 381M-param diffusion world model of CS:GO Dust II (87 h of data, 12 days on one RTX 4090, ~10 Hz on an RTX 3090) but trained **no** agent inside it. The only agent trained purely inside a learned world model of a 3D game and then evaluated in the real game is Dreamer 4 (Minecraft, Sep 2025, 256–1024 TPU-v5p, self-reported preprint). I found no instance of a policy trained inside a CS/FPS world model and transferred back.
- Industry "human-like bot" work (Riot/modl.ai, Activision/NVIDIA MLMove, Tencent Arena Breakout, Epic/Caltech) deliberately avoids pixels and uses ray-casts or game-state inputs, citing compute cost; they are human-like-ness (Turing-test / distribution-match) results, not skill results.

## KQ1. Historical superhuman / human-level results in FPS-like games (FTW, ViZDoom, VPT, GT Sophy) — recipes, compute, and relevance to CS

### Takeaway
Only FTW (Quake III CTF) beat humans from pixels, using ~450k games of population-based RL in an instrumented engine; VPT reached roughly human success rates on Minecraft's diamond-pickaxe task from pixels via 70k h of web video + 0.5B-param BC + RL fine-tuning on 720 V100s; GT Sophy is superhuman but state-based (not pixels). ViZDoom competition bots never beat humans. All relied on a fast, controllable simulator that commercial CS does not offer.

### Cited Findings
- FTW (DeepMind, Science 2019, peer-reviewed; blog is primary summary): agents learn "from raw pixels" acting through an emulated controller with only the win/loss reward; architecture is recurrent nets on fast and slow timescales plus shared memory; population-based training with 30 agents; the Elo chart shows >450K training games; a 40-human tournament found FTW win rates exceeding humans; FTW Elo reaches ~1600, passing average-human Elo at ~100K games and strong-human at ~180K games — [DeepMind blog: Capture the Flag](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/capture-the-flag-the-emergence-of-complex-cooperative-agents/)
- Reaction-time handicap: agents with a 267 ms observation delay still beat humans; strong humans won only 21% of games against delayed agents (intermediate players 12%, exploitability testers 30%); tagging event counts were comparable between delayed agents and humans — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/capture-the-flag-the-emergence-of-complex-cooperative-agents/)
- Secondary press figures: ~450,000 games ≈ "four years of human play" in a few weeks; Elo 1600 vs strong humans 1300 and average 1050 — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/quake-3-bots-play-450000-games-of-capture-the-flag-learn-to-beat-human-teams/); [Synced](https://syncedreview.com/2019/06/06/deepmind-ai-reaches-human-level-performance-in-quake-iii-arena/)
- ViZDoom competitions (2016–2017; arXiv 1809.03470): bots used only the raw screen buffer; top Track-1 bot F1 scored 559 frags and Arnold had best frags/death 1.90; the authors conclude RL bots "are not yet able to successfully compete against humans" — [ViZDoom Competitions paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.03470)
- Sample Factory (2020, ICML, peer-reviewed): APPO throughput >10^5 env frames/s on 3D problems; press coverage reports up to ~130k FPS and that 8 trained agents beat ViZDoom's built-in bots on highest difficulty in 100% of duel/deathmatch matches (bots, not humans) — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/intels-sample-factory-speeds-up-reinforcement-learning-training-on-a-single-pc); [Emergent Mind summary of 2006.11751](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2006.11751); community Sample Factory 2.0 ViZDoom deathmatch checkpoints exist on HF — [doom_deathmatch_bots](https://huggingface.co/edbeeching/doom_deathmatch_bots_1111/blob/main/README.md)
- "Do We Need Transformers to Play FPS Video Games?" (Univ. of Waterloo, arXiv Apr 2025, self-reported): in ViZDoom Team Deathmatch a DQN+DRQN (LSTM) "consistently achieved better performance" than DTQN (5 transformer layers, 8 heads, 50-frame CNN window; ~20 h / 1.5M iterations); offline, PPO beat Decision Transformer (45,000 trajectories, context 90); authors conclude transformers are not well-suited to memory/strategy-intensive ViZDoom tasks and suggest Decision Mamba — [arXiv 2504.17891](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17891v1)
- VPT (OpenAI, NeurIPS 2022, peer-reviewed): IDM trained on 1,962 h of contractor data (≈4,500 h recorded total, ≈$90k; ≈$2,000 of data sufficed for the IDM in most results); IDM accuracy 90.6% keypress, R² 0.97 mouse; ~270k h of web video filtered to ~70k h "web_clean"; foundation BC model 0.5B params trained ~9 days on 720 V100 GPUs; 20 Hz native mouse+keyboard; 640×360 downsampled to 128×128; RL fine-tuned model ~248M params, ~1.3M episodes (~1.4×10^10 frames), phasic policy gradient, 10-minute episodes; results: diamond pickaxe 2.5% (humans 12%), diamonds ~20% (humans 15%), iron pickaxe >80% (humans 57%) — [arXiv 2206.11795 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.11795); abstract — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11795); press on 70k h / $160k contractor spend — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/how-do-you-train-an-ai-to-play-minecraft-feed-it-70000-hours-of-youtube-gameplay); [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2022/07/openai-vpt-minecraft)
- GT Sophy (Sony AI, Nature 2022, peer-reviewed; recipe details from Sony blogs): Peter Wurman: "about 20 PlayStations running simultaneously for about 10 to 12 days to train GT Sophy to race from scratch to superhuman level" — [Sony AI blog: five years on](https://ai.sony/blog/gran-turismo-sophy-five-years-on-from-nature-cover-to-open-frontier); the Nature run reportedly trained >9 more days accumulating >45,000 driving hours before lap-time gains stalled — [GTPlanet](https://www.gtplanet.net/how-gran-turismo-sophy-actually-works-more-details-on-polyphony-digital-and-sony-ais-new-technology/); QR-SAC (distributional SAC) — [Classmethod CEDEC 2022 report](https://dev.classmethod.jp/articles/cedec2022_gran_turismo_sophy_deep_learning_ai/); >1,000 PlayStations were used for automated testing at productization, not training — [Bloomberg via BQ Prime](https://www.bloombergquint.com/business/sony-uses-1-000-playstations-to-train-gran-turismo-ai-champion)
- Tencent "Training Interactive Agent in Large FPS Game Map with Rule-enhanced RL" (Arena Breakout, arXiv Oct 2024, self-reported): inputs are UE4 game-state scalars plus ray-cast depth map [40,80] and circular ray scan [144,3] — no pixels; 9 auto-regressive action heads (Fire 2, Gun_yaw 13, Gun_pitch 8, Move_type 4, Path_type 4, Motion 17, Posture 4, Lean 3, Special 3); PPO in IMPALA-style actor-learner with LSTM; 8 GPUs, 3,200 CPU cores, 6,000 game clients per session; rule-enhanced (navmesh + shooting rules) win rate ~30% higher than pure RL; deployed in Arena Breakout since early 2024 for high-level matches; no human comparison — [arXiv 2410.04936](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04936v1)

### Inferences
- Every superhuman FPS/racing result (FTW, Sophy, Tencent's deployed bot) used an engine-level simulator running thousands of parallel clients or dozens of consoles. Counter-Strike offers no such API (Pearce & Zhu note algorithms "must train and run in real-time"), so FTW-style RL from pixels is not directly reproducible for CS without a Source-2 headless farm.
- VPT's compute (720 V100 × 9 days for BC, ~1.4×10^10 RL frames) is the realistic scale at which pixel-BC+RL reached human-comparable success on a long-horizon task; CS deathmatch is shorter-horizon but demands precise aiming, which none of these landmarks had to solve at human mouse precision (FTW used gamepad-emulated turning in a CTF mode where 267 ms delay still sufficed).

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the FTW Science paper itself (TPU count, frame rate, resolution are not on the blog).
- GT Sophy's exact QR-SAC hyperparameters/reward design were not retrieved (Nature paper paywalled).
- No 2024–2026 ViZDoom result claiming human-level play was found.

## KQ2. Behavioral cloning at scale from video/gameplay (VPT, Pearce & Zhu CS:GO, MLMove, Pixels2Play, NitroGen, Cortex) — data scale, architectures, action parameterizations

### Takeaway
The field moved from ~100 h single-game BC (CS:GO 2021–22, medium-bot level) to 7k–40k h multi-game BC foundation models in 2025–26 (Pixels2Play, NitroGen); architectures are small CNN/EfficientNet or SigLIP/DINOv3 encoders feeding 150M–1.2B decoder-only or diffusion transformers; mouse actions are discretized (quantile or foveated bins) or emitted as continuous chunks via flow matching. None reports human-vs-agent win rates in a shooter.

### Cited Findings
**CS:GO pixel BC baseline (context, 2021–2022)**
- Pearce & Zhu, "Counter-Strike Deathmatch with Large-Scale Behavioural Cloning" (IEEE CoG 2022 Best Paper, peer-reviewed; NeurIPS 2021 Offline RL workshop): pre-trained on scraped online-server play (v2 abstract: 5.5M frames / 95 h; v1: 4M frames), fine-tuned on small expert sets; agent "matches the performance of the medium difficulty built-in AI on the deathmatch game mode" with humanlike style — [arXiv 2104.04258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04258); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9893617/)
- Reported numbers (secondary summary): agent 2.67 kills/min and 1.25 K/D vs built-in bot 1.97 and 1.00; against top humans the agent got 0.5 kills/min and 0.26 K/D vs human average 4.27 and 2.34 — [deeplearning.ai The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/behavioral-cloning-shootout)
- Repo details: scraped dust2 dataset 5,500 .hdf5 files (~700 GB, ~1 min each); expert dust2 190 files (~24 GB); aim-mode expert 45 files (~6 GB); expert nuke/mirage/inferno 10 files each; agent acts 16×/s (32×/s with IS_SPLIT_MOUSE); game at 1024×768 4:3 windowed; large CNN + LSTM; mouse x/y one-hot bins (count not stated); needs CUDA GPU for real-time; datasets and four models on Hugging Face; non-commercial license — [GitHub TeaPearce/Counter-Strike_Behavioural_Cloning](https://github.com/TeaPearce/Counter-Strike_Behavioural_Cloning)
- The Riot/modl.ai paper benchmarks the CS:GO pixel model at 24.10 ms per inference on an i7-10700KF CPU with 5.96M params — [arXiv 2501.00078](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00078v1)

**MLMove (Stanford/Activision/NVIDIA/UW/Cornell; Computer Graphics Forum 2024, peer-reviewed)**
- Input is game state (10 player tokens with position, alive, team, bomb site, time-to-detonation), *not pixels*; the pixel approach of Pearce & Zhu is described as too expensive; 4-layer single-head transformer encoder, d=256, 5.4M params, 21 MB; dataset CSKnow: 123 h at 16 Hz from >17K Retakes rounds, 2,292 players, 513K shots, 29K eliminations, filtered from 1,156 h of HLTV de_dust2 logs (Apr 2021–Nov 2022); training 20 epochs, batch 1024, Adam lr 4e-5, ~1.5 h on an i7-12700K + RTX 4090; inference ~8 ms/query on one Xeon core, run every 16 ticks → ~0.5 ms/tick amortized; movement output is a 97-way categorical (16 directions × 3 speeds × jump + still); **aiming and firing are rule-based**, not learned; TrueSkill human-likeness: MLMove rated 16–59% more human-like than RuleMove/GameBot, humans highest (H=333, p<1e-5); EMD to human play: map occupancy 8.2 vs 14.7/15.2, kill locations 6.7 vs 15.4/16.4; limitations: one map, Retakes only, no grenades — [arXiv 2408.13934](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13934v1); [NVIDIA Research page](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2024-09_learning-move-professional-counter-strike-players); [CGF DOI](https://diglib.eg.org/items/f55b53b1-3785-438b-91f8-1b21d8a4baa9/full); press — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/mlmove-csgo-bot-moves-like-a-professional-player-on-de_dust2/)

**Pixels2Play / Player2 (Elefant AI) — 2025–2026**
- "Pixels to Play: A Foundation Model for 3D Gameplay" (CoG 2025 short paper, peer-reviewed venue; Aug 2025): decoder-only transformer with auto-regressive action output; labeled instrumented demos plus unlabeled public video with actions imputed by an inverse-dynamics model; "latency-friendly on a single consumer GPU"; qualitative competent play in simple Roblox and MS-DOS titles; no quantitative human comparison — [arXiv 2508.14295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14295)
- "Learning to play: A Multimodal Agent for 3D Game-Play" (Player2, Oct 2025, self-reported): ~7,000 h labeled human gameplay plus ~16,000 h unlabeled video, 20 Hz; policy transformer 11 layers × 2048-d, ~400M params total; image tokenizer = first 6 layers of pretrained EfficientNet → 1–4 tokens/frame; up to 4 simultaneous keys + 2 mouse actions decoded autoregressively; trained on 8× H100 (duration not given); real-time 20 Hz on an RTX 5090 with KV cache + sliding-window attention; text conditioning via CLIP (beat Gemma/SigLIP2); Doom "pick up the shotgun" 5/5 vs 1/5 without text — [arXiv 2510.16774](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16774v1)
- "Scaling Behavior Cloning Improves Causal Reasoning: An Open Model for Real-Time Video Game Playing" (Open P2P, Jan 2026, self-reported): ~8,300 h / ~600M image-action pairs after filtering; 44 titles incl. DOOM, Quake, Call of Duty, Need for Speed, Roblox, plus internal Hovercraft and Simple-FPS envs; 20 FPS; correction (DAgger-style) data <1% of dataset; models 150M/300M/600M/1.2B; 192×192 input; 8 action tokens per step (4 keyboard, 2 mouse x/y movement, 2 mouse buttons); mouse movement uses quantile-based bins refined with truncated-normal sampling (σx=96, σy=22); inference on RTX 5090: 80/64/62/40 FPS for 150M/300M/600M/1.2B (target 20 Hz); data scaling law L(D)=1.111+(17/D)^0.2336 fitted on 6–100% of ~500M frames (~7,000 h); "causality score" rises with model and data size; human raters preferred larger models on DOOM/Quake/Roblox clips (exact % only in figure); no numeric DOOM win rates; code/data/checkpoints released — [arXiv 2601.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04575v2); blog: claims "human-level competence" in DOOM and Roblox, action-decoder design gives ~5× inference speedup; GitHub elefant-ai/open-p2p, HF dataset elefantai/p2p-full-data — [Player2 blog](https://blog.player2.game/p/scaling-behavior-cloning-improves)

**NitroGen (NVIDIA / MineDojo; CVPR 2026 oral, peer-reviewed; arXiv Jan 2026)**
- ~40,000 h across >1,000 games (846 games >1 h, 91 games >100 h, 15 games >1,000 h); 71,000 h raw video from 38,739 videos by 818 creators, ~55% kept after filtering; genre skew: action-RPG 34.9%, platformer 18.4%, action-adventure 9.2%; actions are **parsed from on-screen gamepad overlays** (template matching with ~300 controller templates, SIFT/XFeat; fine-tuned SegFormer on consecutive frame pairs predicting 11×11 joystick grid + buttons; trained on 8M synthetic frames; joystick R² 0.84, button accuracy 0.96) — not an IDM; action space is gamepad only: 16 binary buttons + 4 continuous joystick axes (24 values/step); keyboard/mouse games are underrepresented — [arXiv 2601.02427 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427v1); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02427); [CVPR 2026 oral listing](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2026/oral/40365)
- Architecture: GR00T N1 backbone with language/state encoders removed; SigLIP 2 ViT on 256×256 RGB → 256 tokens/frame; diffusion transformer policy trained with conditional flow matching; context of one past frame (more gave no benefit); outputs a 16-action chunk; 16 denoising steps at inference; "NitroGen 500M"; AdamW, lr 1e-4 WSD schedule, EMA 0.9999 — [arXiv 2601.02427 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427v1)
- Evaluation: "universal simulator" wraps commercial Windows games in a Gymnasium API by freezing the game's system clock while the model predicts; benchmark 10 games × 30 tasks (11 combat, 10 navigation, 9 game-specific), human-judged success; fine-tuning on held-out games gives ~10% (isometric roguelike) to ~25% (3D action-RPG) average relative improvement, up to 52% relative in a 30-h low-data regime on combat tasks; described as a "System-1" reactive model with no long-horizon planning or instruction following; dataset, simulator and weights open-sourced — [arXiv 2601.02427 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427v1); weights — [HF nvidia/NitroGen](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NitroGen)

**Frozen-feature compact BC (2026)**
- "Cortex: Compact Behavior Cloning for Quake with Frozen Visual Features" (single author D. Malyshau, arXiv Jul 2026, self-reported): frozen **DINOv3** encoder + 6-layer transformer, 10.98M trainable params; trained on the Quake subset of Pixels2Play (6,849 recordings, ~474.7 h, 17.09M cached decision frames); one sampled epoch of 517k four-frame windows takes 3.3 min of policy-head optimization on one RTX 5080 (feature extraction excluded); on Quake E1M1, 19/20 120-s episodes record ≥1 kill and all reach the gate descent, but it never completes the level; released P2P-150M and NitroGen checkpoints went shallower in 5 matched episodes each (small samples, differing interfaces); denser visual tokens improve combat; remaining failures attributed to covariate shift — [arXiv 2607.22739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22739)

**Action-parameterization inventory (from the above)**
- VPT: 20 Hz native mouse+keyboard; discretized mouse (bin details in App. C.2, not read) — [arXiv 2206.11795](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.11795)
- Dreamer 4 (Minecraft): 23 binary keyboard actions + 121-class categorical mouse with foveated discretization — [arXiv 2509.24527](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527v1)
- Open P2P: quantile bins for mouse dx/dy, 8 tokens/step — [arXiv 2601.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04575v2)
- NitroGen: continuous 4-D joystick + 16 buttons, 16-step action chunks via flow-matching diffusion policy — [arXiv 2601.02427](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427v1)
- Riot/modl.ai: yaw×pitch as a single 11×15=165-way discrete choice aligned to the ray-cast angles — [arXiv 2501.00078](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00078v1)
- Tencent Arena Breakout: separate Gun_yaw (13) and Gun_pitch (8) heads — [arXiv 2410.04936](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04936v1)
- MLMove: 97-way movement categorical; aim rule-based — [arXiv 2408.13934](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13934v1)

### Inferences
- The data scale that produced "medium bot" CS play was ~100 h; the 2026 open models have ~80× more data (across games) and show monotone loss/causality improvements with data (P2P scaling law), but there is still no demonstrated pixel-BC agent that beats a decent human in any deathmatch-style shooter — human-level-in-shooter claims are qualitative.
- Pretrained visual encoders (SigLIP 2 in NitroGen, DINOv3 in Cortex) have replaced from-scratch CNNs; Cortex shows that with frozen DINOv3 features a 10M-param head trains in minutes per epoch on a consumer GPU, which is the lowest-cost credible 2026 recipe for a new FPS.
- Diffusion/flow-matching action heads with chunking (NitroGen) are the game-agent import of robotics "diffusion policy"; the P2P line stays with autoregressive discrete tokens. Neither has a published head-to-head on mouse-aim precision.

### Gaps
- Pixels2Play papers do not report training GPU-days (only "8× H100"), nor numeric win rates in any FPS.
- NitroGen omits training compute, inference Hz, and any FPS-specific (Doom/Quake) numbers; it is gamepad-only so not directly a keyboard-mouse recipe.
- I could not verify Pearce & Zhu's backbone (often described as EfficientNet) or mouse bin count from accessible text.
- No 2025–2026 large-scale video-pretrained *FPS* agent from Meta, DeepMind (non-LLM), NetEase or miHoYo was found; Tencent's FPS work is state-based RL (Arena Breakout) or LLM behavior trees (PORTAL, arXiv 2503.13356).

## KQ3. World models and model-based RL (DreamerV3/Dreamer 4, DIAMOND incl. CS:GO, GameNGen, Oasis, Genie 2/3, WHAM/Muse, SCOPE, GameWAM) — has a policy been trained inside a CS/FPS world model and transferred?

### Takeaway
Learned world models of FPS footage now exist (DIAMOND CS:GO 2024, GameNGen Doom 2024, SCOPE cross-game FPS 2026, WHAM Bleeding Edge 2025) but **none of them trained a policy inside the model and deployed it to the real game**. The one 3D-game result that does so is Dreamer 4 (Minecraft, Sep 2025): a ~2B-param world model trained on 2,541 h of VPT data, policy trained purely in imagination, 0.7% diamond rate / 29% iron-pickaxe rate in the real game.

### Cited Findings
- DreamerV3 (Nature, published 2 Apr 2025, peer-reviewed): single fixed configuration across >150 tasks; first algorithm to collect Minecraft diamonds from sparse rewards without human data or curricula; first diamond at 30M environment steps (17 days of in-game playtime) — [danijar.com DreamerV3 page](https://danijar.com/project/dreamerv3/); [arXiv 2301.04104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104); Scientific American reports ~9 days of continuous play to find a diamond and a dense +1 reward for each of 12 intermediate milestones (a caveat on the "no curricula" framing), with Hafner quoted that the diamond result was an afterthought — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/google-deepmind-taught-itself-to-play-minecraft)
- DIAMOND (NeurIPS 2024, peer-reviewed): Atari 100k mean HNS 1.46, IQM 0.64, 11/26 games superhuman, ~2.9 days per run on one RTX 4090; **CS:GO**: trained on Pearce & Zhu's Online dataset, 5.5M frames (95 h) at 16 Hz, 87 h train / 8 h held out; frames reduced 280×150 → 56×30 for the dynamics model plus a separate diffusion upsampler; 381M params total (51M upsampler), scaled from 4M for Atari; 12 days on one RTX 4090; ~10 Hz on an RTX 3090 with 3 denoising steps (10 for the upsampler); "no RL agent or online data collection" — the CS:GO world model was trained on static data only — [arXiv 2405.12399](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12399); press — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/pc-gaming/counter-strikes-dust-ii-runs-purely-within-a-neural-network-on-an-rtx-3090-performance-is-disappointing-at-only-10-fps); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-model-simulates-counter-strike-with-10-fps-on-a-single-rtx-3090/)
- GameNGen (Google, ICLR 2025, peer-reviewed): DOOM simulated at 20 FPS on a single TPU; training data recorded from an RL agent playing DOOM; human raters only slightly better than chance distinguishing short real vs simulated clips; no agent trained inside the model — [arXiv 2408.14837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14837); [ICLR 2025 proceedings PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/b71ecea210f7159f31e46631fe5c838f-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Oasis (Decart/Etched, Oct 31 2024, self-reported): diffusion-transformer Minecraft world model, 500M-param open weights, 20 FPS at 360p, trained on VPT data on H100s; criticized for poor persistence — [oasis-model.github.io](https://oasis-model.github.io); [HF Etched/oasis-500m](https://huggingface.co/Etched/oasis-500m); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/ai-powered-minecraft-runs-without-a-game-engine-game-rendered-in-real-time-at-a-continuous-20-fps)
- Genie 2 (DeepMind, 4 Dec 2024, self-reported blog): autoregressive latent diffusion foundation world model; keyboard+mouse action per step; worlds consistent up to ~1 minute (most examples 10–20 s); SIMA agent tested inside (e.g., open red/blue door); distilled real-time variant at reduced quality — [DeepMind Genie 2 blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/genie-2-a-large-scale-foundation-world-model/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/deepminds-genie-2-creates-playable-3d-worlds-from-single-images/)
- Genie 3 (DeepMind, Aug 2025, self-reported blog): 720p at 24 FPS in real time; consistency for "a few minutes"; ~1 minute of visual memory; SIMA agent drove navigation actions inside generated worlds; limitations: limited action space, poor multi-agent modeling, few-minute horizon; limited research preview only — [DeepMind Genie 3 blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/)
- SIMA 2 (DeepMind, 13 Nov 2025; Gemini-based, so outside the non-LLM scope but relevant as the only reported agent "self-improving" inside Genie 3 worlds): learns new tasks in Genie-generated environments with Gemini-provided task and reward estimates, no extra human data; limitations include "precise, low-level keyboard and mouse actions" — [DeepMind SIMA 2 blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/sima-2-an-agent-that-plays-reasons-and-learns-with-you-in-virtual-3d-worlds/); secondary figures: 65% task success vs SIMA 1's 31% vs human 71% — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/deepminds-latest-ai-agent-learns-by-exploring-unfamiliar-games-and-ai-built-worlds/)
- WHAM / Muse (Microsoft Research, Nature Feb 2025, peer-reviewed; details from model card): Ninja Theory's Bleeding Edge (4v4), ~500,000 games on 7 maps, >1 billion observation-action pairs at 10 Hz (~7 years of play), one year of anonymized data from 27,990 players; checkpoints 200M (3.7 GB) and 1.6B (18.9 GB); VQ-GAN tokenizer + decoder-only transformer over interleaved observation/action tokens; 300×180 px; context 10 (obs, action) pairs = 5,560 tokens ≈ 10 s; trained on 98 H100 GPUs for 5 days; three modes incl. a **behavior-policy mode** (actions from visuals); evaluated on consistency (FVD), diversity (Wasserstein over actions), persistency; Microsoft Research License (academic only) — [HF microsoft/wham](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/wham); secondary — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/microsofts-new-ai-model-muse-can-generate-gameplay-and-might-preserve-classic-games/); [GitHub mirror dweam-team/wham](https://github.com/dweam-team/wham)
- Dreamer 4, "Training Agents Inside of Scalable World Models" (Hafner, Yan, Lillicrap; arXiv 29 Sep 2025, self-reported preprint): VPT contractor dataset ≈2,541 h of 360p video with mouse/keyboard at 20 FPS; ~2B params (≈400M tokenizer + 1.6B dynamics); 256 spatial tokens, 192-frame context; shortcut-forcing objective with K=4 forward passes per frame; trained on 256–1024 TPU-v5p (duration not stated); claims real-time interactive inference on a single GPU; policy = BC with multi-token prediction (length 8) on 20 one-hot tasks, then imagination RL with PMPO (α=0.5, β=0.3, γ=0.997) updating only policy/value heads with frozen transformer; **results in real Minecraft (1,000 × 60-min episodes): diamonds 0.7%, iron pickaxe 29% (vs 11% for a Gemma-3 VLA baseline), stone pickaxe >90%; fine-tuned VPT reaches sticks 53%**; ~100× less data than VPT; human testers could complete block placement inside Dreamer 4 but not inside Oasis or Lucid — [arXiv 2509.24527 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527v1); [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24527)
- SCOPE (UCAS-Terminus / Tencent internship et al., arXiv 28 May 2026, self-reported): CrossFPS dataset of 69,000 five-second clips (~96 h by my arithmetic) from 7 FPS titles (Halo Infinite, Xonotic, CoD: MW, Halo, CoD: Warzone, CoD: MWIII, CoD) with frame-aligned 10-DoF controller telemetry (4 continuous axes, 6 buttons), sourced from NitroGen and WorldCam; conditioning modules inserted into all 30 blocks of Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (~5B params); 480×832, 81 frames (5 s @ 20 fps); ~18 h on 8 NVIDIA GPUs; action completion on unseen scenes 71.5% vs 38.3% LingBot-World (Act), 8.0% HY-World 1.5, 0.5% Matrix-Game 3.0; **no agents/policies trained inside; long-horizon task execution is future work**; code/model/data links given — [arXiv 2605.23345](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23345)
- GameWAM, "A World Action Model for Video Games" (arXiv Aug 2026, self-reported): jointly generates future frames and executable keyboard-mouse trajectories with parallel visual/action flow-matching and block-causal conditioning; block-cycle control (predict → execute short action block → replan); claims competitive task success with fewer native actions; games and model size unspecified on abstract page — [arXiv 2608.26200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26200)
- Other 2026 FPS-adjacent world-model preprints surfaced (not read in full): ForgeWM few-step causal world model, says recipe transfers to gamepad FPS — [arXiv 2608.14022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14022); Game2World Engine (in-the-wild gameplay video for WM training) — [arXiv 2608.24680](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24680); StatePlay — [arXiv 2607.26754](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26754); Solaris multiplayer Minecraft WM — [arXiv 2602.22208](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.22208); survey "AI for Games in the Foundation Model Era" — [arXiv 2609.16679](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16679)
- A 2026 world-model survey table lists the 2018 Ha & Schmidhuber "World Models" (VizDoom take-cover) as the classic case of imagined-to-real transfer in an FPS-like env (VAE+RNN, not diffusion) — [arXiv 2606.00133](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.00133)

### Inferences
- Imagination-RL for a CS-like game is technically plausible in 2026 — DIAMOND shows an 87-h CS:GO world model fits on one 4090 in 12 days, and Dreamer 4 shows the policy-in-imagination loop works for a 3D keyboard-mouse game — but nobody has closed the loop for an FPS. The blockers visible in the sources are horizon/consistency (Genie 3 "a few minutes", WHAM ~10 s context), the need for action-labeled data at scale, and compute for Dreamer-4-class models (hundreds of TPUs).
- FPS world models so far model *video* fidelity (SCOPE, GameNGen) or are gamepad-conditioned; none models hit-registration/combat outcomes well enough to be a training ground for aiming, so the "practice in the dream" story remains unproven for shooters.

### Gaps
- Nature DreamerV3 methods (parameter sizes, GPUs, replay ratio) were not retrievable (paywall); the SciAm "12 intermediate rewards" claim is secondary.
- Dreamer 4 reports no training wall-clock, no specific inference GPU/FPS.
- NVIDIA Cosmos was not investigated; I found no source applying Cosmos to FPS agents.
- No source answers whether any lab has trained an FPS policy inside a learned world model and deployed it; absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

## KQ4. RL fine-tuning after BC (PPO/IMPALA/PPG from BC init, RL in imagination) — sample efficiency and wall-clock when the environment runs in real time

### Takeaway
The only quantified BC→RL pipelines for pixel keyboard-mouse agents are VPT (≈1.3M 10-minute episodes ≈ 1.4×10^10 frames of PPG on a 248M-param policy, feasible only because Minecraft runs headless and parallel) and Dreamer 4 (RL entirely in imagination, zero real-game interaction). For CS2 — no API, anti-cheat, real-time only — no published RL fine-tune exists; industry shooters that use RL (Tencent) do it in-engine with thousands of clients and state inputs.

### Cited Findings
- VPT RL fine-tuning: ~248M-param model, ~1.3M episodes (~1.4×10^10 frames), phasic policy gradient, 10-min episodes; diamond pickaxe 2.5% vs 12% for humans; diamonds ~20% vs 15%; iron pickaxe >80% vs 57% — [arXiv 2206.11795](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.11795)
- Dreamer 4 policy training: BC multi-token (8) then PMPO imagination RL, one rollout per context, frozen transformer; no environment interaction at all; iron pickaxe 29% vs 11% VLA baseline — [arXiv 2509.24527](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527v1)
- Tencent Arena Breakout: PPO+GAE+LSTM in IMPALA-style actor-learner; 8 GPUs, 3,200 CPU cores, 4 actors, 4 learners, 6,000 clients per session; rule-enhanced RL ~30% higher win rate than pure RL at equal timesteps; reward +20 win / −20 loss / −25 draw + 0.05×distance navigation + auxiliary rewards from expert priors; no training duration reported — [arXiv 2410.04936](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04936v1)
- EA SEED (Battlefield 1, 2018, context): imitation learning gave a "4x improvement in training speed" with IL used only briefly at the start, after which movement "ends up being remarkably similar to that of human gameplay" — [EA SEED](https://www.ea.com/seed/news/seed-imitation-learning-concurrent-actions); [arXiv 1803.05402](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.05402v5)
- Riot/modl.ai human-like bots: pure supervised BC (lr 3e-4, batch 96, 600 epochs, seq len 64); RL and GAIL are listed as future work, not done — [arXiv 2501.00078](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00078v1)
- NitroGen's "universal simulator" freezes the commercial game's system clock while the model predicts, converting a real-time Windows game into a step-able Gymnasium env; authors argue this doesn't alter physics — [arXiv 2601.02427](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427v1)
- Pearce & Zhu: "no API is available for CSGO, so algorithms must train and run in real-time" — [arXiv 2104.04258 (via search excerpt)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.04258)
- General 2025 BC→RL fine-tuning literature (robotics, not games) emphasizes that wall-clock, not samples, is the bottleneck: ResFiT learns residual corrections over a frozen BC base with off-policy RL — [arXiv 2509.19301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19301); Posterior Behavioral Cloning shapes the BC pretrain for faster RL fine-tune — [arXiv 2512.16911](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16911); TMRL/"Turning the Dial" modulates diffusion timestep for exploration, real-world fine-tune "in under an hour" — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=fuySiHtsuo); world-model-assisted BC fine-tuning with 100K env-step budget — [NeurIPS 2024](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/101647)
- Hobbyist CS:GO RL attempt (CSGOMLBot) gathers screenshots on headless servers inside a virtual display; no finished agent shown — [GitHub Trevato/CSGOMLBot](https://github.com/Trevato/CSGOMLBot)

### Inferences
- Back-of-envelope (my arithmetic): VPT's 1.4×10^10 RL frames at 20 Hz = 7×10^8 s ≈ 22 years of real-time play; even 1% of that is impossible for one real-time CS2 client, so RL fine-tuning for CS2 must happen either (a) with many parallel headless clients (Tencent-style, requires engine access), (b) in a learned world model (Dreamer 4-style), or (c) as very low-sample residual/off-policy correction on top of a strong BC policy (ResFiT-style, untested in games).
- NitroGen's clock-freezing trick is the most practical known way to make a commercial game step-able for evaluation/fine-tuning on a single PC, but it does not provide parallelism and may interact badly with online anti-cheat.

### Gaps
- No paper reports wall-clock or sample counts for RL fine-tuning of a pixel BC agent in any commercial FPS (CS, Valorant, CoD). I found no "RLHF-like" or "RL from human play" FPS paper in 2024–2026.
- Tencent does not report training duration or human-vs-bot results.

## KQ5. Human-like bots research (arXiv 2501.00078 and industry work: Riot, Activision, EA, Ubisoft, Epic, Tencent) and 2025–2026 follow-ups

### Takeaway
Industry human-like-bot work converges on BC from tens to hundreds of hours of human play with cheap non-pixel observations (ray-casts, game-state tokens), small models (0.6M–36M params) that run in a few ms on CPU, and evaluation via Turing-style clip studies and distribution matching; none of them aims for superhuman skill and all explicitly reject pixels on compute grounds. I found no 2025–2026 follow-up paper from Riot/modl.ai or Activision.

### Cited Findings
- Riot Games + modl.ai, "Human-like Bots for Tactical Shooters Using Compute-Efficient Sensors" (arXiv 30 Dec 2024, self-reported preprint; authors incl. Niels Justesen, Julian Togelius, Sebastian Risi (modl.ai), Wesley Kerr and Albert Wang (Riot)): game is "Lyra:Ascent", a VALORANT-inspired 2v2 built on UE5 Lyra; observations: 225 ray casts in a 15×15 grid (dense near crosshair) → 15×15×10 tensor, 100 m range; extra rays to important objects within 90° FOV; 8 directional audio sensors × 5 sound types; ~50 game-state features; actions: aim as one 11×15=165-way discrete yaw/pitch aligned to ray angles, plus WASD/jump/plant-defuse/drop/reload/2 abilities/fire; data: 28 players, 48.6 h at 16 Hz = 2,797,632 timesteps from LAN sessions; architecture: parallel encoders (3×3 conv on ray grid, dense elsewhere) → LSTM (dropout 0.5) → multi-head outputs; six variants 0.63M–36.4M params; training lr 3e-4, batch 96, 600 epochs, seq len 64, no RL; CPU inference (i7-10700KF): 1.95 ms (model A) to 18.78 ms (36.4M-param model F) vs 24.10 ms for the CS:GO pixel baseline (5.96M params); human study: 20 gamers × 32 fifteen-second clips — human clips judged human 60%, bot clips judged human 30% (bot 54%, unsure 16%); limitations: bots move faster than humans, larger models fire less, one map/one weapon/two roles — [arXiv 2501.00078](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00078v1); [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00078v1); [alphaXiv overview](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2501.00078v1)
- MLMove (Activision/NVIDIA/Stanford, CGF 2024): see KQ2; human-likeness TrueSkill +16–59% vs commercial GameBot and RuleMove; teamwork error (offense flanking) 27% vs 55%/58%; defense spreading 13% vs 42%/87% — [arXiv 2408.13934](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13934v1)
- Epic Games / Caltech, "Humanlike Behavior in a Third-Person Shooter with Imitation Learning" (Farhang, Mulcahy, Holden, Matthews, Yue; IEEE CoG, Milan, Aug 2024, peer-reviewed): identity-conditioned causal autoregressive transformer with a learned per-player (or per-skill-bucket) identity token; controlled 1v1 gym for aiming and target acquisition; claims to replicate qualitative and quantitative aspects of humanlike play and individual style; metrics not on the companion page — [project page](https://alexfarhang.github.io/humanlikebehavior); [PDF](https://alexfarhang.github.io/assets/pdf/Humanlike_Behavior.pdf); search snippet indicates a custom Fortnite/UE5 1v1 map vs a behavior-tree bot (unverified from the page itself)
- Tencent Arena Breakout (2024): human-likeness assessed qualitatively via bullet-impact distributions (human clustered; rule-enhanced RL similar with more dispersion; pure RL erratic); deployed since early 2024 — [arXiv 2410.04936](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04936v1)
- Ubisoft La Forge: Rainbow Six Siege team worked with La Forge on an ML framework "to make bots behave more like actual players" (c. 2022) — [Ubisoft News](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/1MlKnolSLJFuJDnATWiorr/how-rainbow-six-siege-developed-ai-that-acts-like-real-players); 2025 publication list includes "Efficient Active Imitation Learning with Random Network Distillation" (ICLR 2025) plus offline-RL/navigation papers, none shooter-specific per the listing — [La Forge publications](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/laforge/publications)
- EA SEED: 2025 item is RL for human-like goalkeepers in EA SPORTS FC 26 (sports, not shooter); SEED's ML test-agent work for AAA testing — [EA SEED ML for game testing](https://www.ea.com/seed/news/seed-ml-research-aaa-game-testing)
- AAAI 2025 student abstract "Towards Building Human-like Smart Agents in Modern 3D Video Games" (Sun et al.) exists; not read — [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/35305)
- Hobby CS2 "AI bots" on GitHub are YOLO-based triggerbots/aim assists (TheLlamainator/CS2-AI-Bot, Priler/csgobot), i.e., cheats, not learned policies — [CS2-AI-Bot](https://github.com/TheLlamainator/CS2-AI-Bot); [csgobot](https://github.com/Priler/csgobot)

### Inferences
- Industry's definition of success is "indistinguishable in a 15-second clip" and "cheap enough to run 10 bots on a server CPU"; this is orthogonal to the "beat humans from pixels" goal and explains why every studio paper drops pixels. A CS2 pixel agent built for human-likeness would be evaluated the same way (TrueSkill rankings, JS-divergence to human feature distributions, EMD on heatmaps).
- Riot's result (30% of bot clips judged human vs 60% of human clips judged human) is the clearest quantitative Turing-test datapoint for a tactical shooter and is far from parity.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 follow-up from Riot/modl.ai or Activision was found; I found no Valorant-specific production bot paper.
- Farhang et al. paper body (dataset hours, metrics, inputs) could not be extracted (PDF only).

## KQ6. Aim-specific learning — neural aiming/recoil control from pixels, reaction-time modeling, high-Hz mouse control

### Takeaway
There is no peer-reviewed paper that learns recoil control or spray patterns from pixels; aiming in published agents is either a coarse discretized yaw/pitch head (165-way, 13×8, 121-way foveated), a rule-based module (MLMove, Tencent's shooting rules), or an implicit part of end-to-end BC at 16–20 Hz. Reaction-time modeling appears only as FTW's 267 ms delay experiment and Riot's 16-Hz human-like policies.

### Cited Findings
- FTW: 267 ms observation delay (human-comparable) still beat strong humans (humans won 21%) — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/capture-the-flag-the-emergence-of-complex-cooperative-agents/)
- Pearce & Zhu collected a dedicated "aim training mode" expert set (45 files, ~6 GB) and note aim mode trains "aim, recoil control and reaction speed"; agent decision rate 16 Hz, 32 Hz with split-mouse — [GitHub TeaPearce](https://github.com/TeaPearce/Counter-Strike_Behavioural_Cloning); [arXiv 2104.04258 (search excerpt)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.04258)
- Tencent: shooting handled by rules — baseline firing point set by body-part visibility priority; shots sampled in a confidence region with baseline angle arctan(40/1500) plus an extra region of radius r/3 to imitate burst fire — [arXiv 2410.04936](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04936v1)
- Riot/modl.ai: aim as a single 165-way discrete yaw×pitch action aligned to ray angles (pitch trimmed to 11 values from human distributions) — [arXiv 2501.00078](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00078v1)
- MLMove: aim via rule-based occupancy map with smoothed view updates; fire when crosshair is on enemy bbox — [arXiv 2408.13934](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13934v1)
- Dreamer 4: 121-class foveated mouse discretization — [arXiv 2509.24527](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527v1); Open P2P: quantile mouse bins with truncated-normal refinement σx=96, σy=22 — [arXiv 2601.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04575v2)
- Epic/Caltech: identity-conditioned causal transformer for aiming/target acquisition in 1v1 — [project page](https://alexfarhang.github.io/humanlikebehavior)
- Older hybrid approach: CNN localizes enemy from pixels, separate neuroevolved net aims and shoots (proof of concept) — [DLNE paper (core.ac.uk)](https://core.ac.uk/download/161653184.pdf)
- Human-like mouse trajectory generation (not FPS): DMTG entropy-controlled diffusion trained on 120 participants (Oct 2024) — [arXiv 2410.18233](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18233v1); WGAN-GP/LSTM synthetic trajectories (2025 thesis) — [DiVA](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:2003088/FULLTEXT01.pdf); HCI studies of FPS targeting (mouse sensitivity) — [arXiv 2203.12050](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12050); wavelet submovement detection (2026) — [arXiv 2604.20673](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.20673)
- CS2 computer-vision aim tools exist as hobby projects (YOLOv8 triggerbot with recoil pattern; CSMLC player detector trained on an RTX 3070) — these are cheats with ban risk, not research — [CS2-AI-Bot](https://github.com/TheLlamainator/CS2-AI-Bot); [CSMLC](https://github.com/SpikeHD/CSMLC)

### Inferences
- The 16–20 Hz decision rates universal in this literature (VPT, Pearce, P2P, Riot, MLMove) are an order of magnitude below the 125–1000 Hz polling of competitive mice; no paper studies whether sub-50 ms mouse control is needed to reach human CS aim quality from pixels. This is an open experimental question.
- Recoil compensation is a deterministic per-weapon pattern in CS; every published approach either ignores it (BC at 16 Hz implicitly averages it) or hard-codes it (Tencent, hobby bots). A learned pixel policy would need either higher Hz or an action-chunking head (NitroGen-style) to express spray control.

### Gaps
- No published work on learned recoil control, no reaction-time distribution modeling for FPS bots beyond FTW's delay ablation, no >32 Hz learned mouse controller for any FPS.

## KQ7. What the practitioner community (GitHub/blogs, 2024–2026) says works on consumer GPUs

- Sample Factory 2.0 ViZDoom deathmatch checkpoints shared on HF (e.g., mean_reward 85.66 ± 28.53 on doom_deathmatch_bots) — [HF edbeeching](https://huggingface.co/edbeeching/doom_deathmatch_bots_1111/blob/main/README.md); [HF andrewzhang505](https://huggingface.co/andrewzhang505/doom_deathmatch_bots/blob/1db418a782e1bfb009ebe4922be3ac279da4b7f5/README.md)
- Stanford CS230 (2021) CS:GO project: YOLOv5 enemy detection + imitation, MSS screen capture, ~4,000 images / ~7 h gameplay — [CS230 report](http://cs230.stanford.edu/projects_fall_2021/reports/102988723.pdf)

### Inferences
- The practitioner consensus visible in these artifacts: (1) sync screen+input capture at 15–20 Hz is the first and hardest engineering gate; (2) use a frozen pretrained encoder and a small head to train on one GPU; (3) expect covariate shift and plan DAgger-style correction data; (4) CS2's anti-cheat forces evaluation on offline/bot servers, which also rules out online RL.

### Gaps
- I found no Hacker News or Reddit threads specifically on Pixels2Play, NitroGen or CS2 agents (searches returned none); no 2025–2026 practitioner blog documenting a CS2 agent beyond the Agentic-CS2 README.

## KQ8 (objective). Current best practice (2026) for a pixels-in / keyboard-mouse-out agent for a game like Counter-Strike

### Takeaway
The evidence-backed 2026 recipe is: large-scale BC from synchronized screen+input recordings (hundreds to thousands of hours), a pretrained ViT/DINOv3 or EfficientNet encoder feeding a 100M–1B transformer at 16–20 Hz, discretized mouse deltas (quantile/foveated bins) or chunked flow-matching actions, DAgger-style correction data, and — if skill beyond human-imitation is wanted — RL either in-engine with parallel headless clients or in imagination inside a learned world model; no one has demonstrated the last step for CS, and anti-cheat constrains evaluation to offline servers.

### Cited Findings (recipe components with their evidentiary source)
- Data: 95 h → medium-bot level (CS:GO, peer-reviewed) — [arXiv 2104.04258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04258); 7k–8.3k h multi-game → qualitative human-like FPS play, loss still falling as a power law in data — [arXiv 2601.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04575v2); 40k h gamepad → +10–52% fine-tune transfer to unseen games — [arXiv 2601.02427](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427v1); 2,541 h action-labeled suffices for a 2B world model + imagination RL in Minecraft — [arXiv 2509.24527](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527v1)
- Encoder: frozen DINOv3 + 11M head (Cortex) — [arXiv 2607.22739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22739); SigLIP 2 ViT (NitroGen) — [arXiv 2601.02427](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427v1); EfficientNet first 6 layers → 1–4 tokens (P2P) — [arXiv 2510.16774](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16774v1)
- Action head: 8 discrete tokens/step with quantile mouse bins (P2P) — [arXiv 2601.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04575v2); 16-step flow-matching chunks (NitroGen) — [arXiv 2601.02427](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427v1); 121-class foveated mouse (Dreamer 4) — [arXiv 2509.24527](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527v1)
- Real-time budget: 20 Hz on RTX 5090 for up to 1.2B params — [arXiv 2601.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04575v2); 16 Hz CNN+LSTM on an "average gaming GPU" (2022) — [GitHub TeaPearce](https://github.com/TeaPearce/Counter-Strike_Behavioural_Cloning)
- Correction data: <1% DAgger-style corrections in P2P — [arXiv 2601.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04575v2); covariate shift is the dominant failure in Cortex — [arXiv 2607.22739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22739)
- RL beyond BC: VPT-scale PPG (1.4×10^10 frames) — [arXiv 2206.11795](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.11795); Tencent in-engine PPO with 6,000 clients — [arXiv 2410.04936](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04936v1); Dreamer 4 imagination RL — [arXiv 2509.24527](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527v1)
- World-model feasibility for CS specifically: 381M-param DIAMOND CS:GO model, 12 days on one RTX 4090, 10 Hz on RTX 3090, no agent trained — [arXiv 2405.12399](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12399)
- Evaluation protocol for human-likeness: TrueSkill clip rankings + EMD/JS-divergence to human distributions (MLMove, Riot) — [arXiv 2408.13934](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13934v1); [arXiv 2501.00078](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00078v1)
- Constraint: no CS API, real-time only, VAC risk → offline bot servers — [arXiv 2104.04258 excerpt](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.04258); [Agentic-CS2](https://github.com/kvsznmate/Agentic-CS2)

### Inferences
- A realistic single-GPU plan in 2026: (1) fine-tune Open P2P 300M–600M (already trained on DOOM/Quake/CoD) on a few hundred hours of CS2 Dust II deathmatch recorded at 20 Hz with synced inputs and GSI labels; (2) add 1–5% corrective (DAgger) data; (3) expect roughly built-in-bot level, consistent with the only peer-reviewed CS datapoint; (4) to exceed that, the credible path is RL in a learned world model (DIAMOND-style CS model exists; Dreamer 4-style policy learning does not yet for FPS) or a headless Source 2 server farm, neither demonstrated.
- Human-level CS aim from pixels is unsolved and unpublished; the only "human-level FPS from pixels" claims in 2025–2026 (Pixels2Play) are self-reported and about DOOM/Roblox, not a modern tactical shooter.

### Gaps
- No controlled comparison exists between discrete-bin, continuous-chunk and diffusion action heads for mouse aiming in any FPS.
- No published result on fine-tuning P2P/NitroGen on Counter-Strike; the CS2 recipe above is an inference assembled from adjacent results.

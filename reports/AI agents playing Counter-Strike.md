# Counter-Strike AI Plateaus at Casual-Human Deathmatch

No AI agent playing Counter-Strike from pixels has credibly beaten human players at any documented skill level as of November 2026, and the measured ceiling is still the 2021–2022 Pearce & Zhu behavioural-cloning agent, which roughly matched CS:GO's medium built-in bot and a casual human in Dust2 deathmatch while posting a **0.59 kill/death ratio against humans** ([ar5iv 2104.04258](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.04258)). Progress since then has been real but lateral: the 2024–2026 literature produced a 381M-parameter diffusion world model of CS:GO (DIAMOND) that nobody has trained an agent inside, a state-based humanlike movement policy (MLMove), four terabyte-scale CS2 egocentric datasets rendered from pro demos (one already removed after a Valve takedown), and open multi-game behavioural-cloning foundation models (Open Pixels2Play, NitroGen) that play DOOM and Quake competently but have never been fine-tuned on Counter-Strike. Frontier LLM/VLM agents are a dead end for the inner loop: every benchmark that keeps the game clock running during inference scores them near zero on shooters, with Doom II at **0% for every model** on VideoGameBench and GPT-5.5 taking **~16 s per decision** on OmniGameArena ([arXiv 2505.18134](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134v3); [arXiv 2606.09826](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09826v1)). The best-supported build for an offline, educational, pixels-in/keyboard-mouse-out CS2 agent is therefore large-scale behavioural cloning at ~20 Hz, initialised from the open Open-P2P 300M–600M checkpoint (or a frozen DINOv3 encoder with a small transformer head on a tighter budget), fine-tuned on hundreds of hours of synchronised CS2 Dust2 deathmatch recordings plus OpenCS2 pretraining data, with DAgger-style corrections, an optional ~1 Hz VLM planner issuing skill chunks, and evaluation on a LAN dedicated server under `-insecure` with FTW-style fairness handicaps (≈250 ms observation delay, action-rate cap, pro-vetted mouse-speed envelope). The honest expectation is built-in-bot to casual-human level; exceeding that requires reinforcement learning either inside a learned world model or across a farm of parallel game clients, and neither has been demonstrated for any commercial FPS.

## Pearce & Zhu's 2021 agent still marks the ceiling for pixel-based Counter-Strike play

The only pixels-in, keyboard/mouse-out Counter-Strike agent with a published quantitative evaluation is "Counter-Strike Deathmatch with Large-Scale Behavioural Cloning," presented at the NeurIPS 2021 Offline RL workshop and published at IEEE Conference on Games 2022, where it won a Best Paper award ([arXiv 2104.04258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04258); [GitHub README](https://github.com/TeaPearce/Counter-Strike_Behavioural_Cloning)). The recipe is instructive because nothing has superseded it for CS specifically: an ImageNet-initialised EfficientNetB0 trunk feeding a convolutional LSTM, consuming a 1024×768 game window cropped and downsampled to 280×150, emitting discretised mouse deltas in **19×13 bins** at **16 forward passes per second**, pre-trained on **5.5M frames (95 h, 680 GB)** of deathmatch scraped from Valve servers and fine-tuned on ~3.3 h of clean expert play ([ar5iv 2104.04258](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.04258)). Training took 4× Titan X per model for 2–5 days each, and inference ran at 16 Hz "on an average gaming GPU."

The results define the state of the art. Against medium built-in bots the fine-tuned agent scored **3.72 kills/min and 2.09 K/D**, against humans **1.43 KPM and 0.59 K/D**; the paper's own human references were a non-gamer at 2.38/0.90 against medium bots, a casual gamer (<100 h CS:GO) at 3.51/2.48 against bots and 1.64/0.64 against humans, and a top-10% "strong" player at **7.80/4.33 against bots and 4.27/2.34 against humans** ([ar5iv 2104.04258](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.04258)). Read against those anchors, the agent sits between a non-gamer and a casual player, at roughly half the kill rate of a strong player, and it dies more than it kills against humans. Its documented weaknesses — poor memory, weak vertical aim, getting stuck, zero-shot kill rates of 0.19–0.46 KPM on other maps — have never been addressed in a follow-up; the repo has 38 commits and SOTA-Verified lists the paper as "not yet reproduced by others" ([sotaverified.org](https://sotaverified.org/papers/counter-strike-deathmatch-with-large-scale)). Every later CS paper reuses its dataset rather than its agent.

The three most-cited 2024 CS results are each something other than a pixel agent. MLMove (Stanford/Activision/NVIDIA, Computer Graphics Forum 2024) is a **5.4M-parameter transformer that outputs movement only** from ten game-state tokens, with aim and fire handled by rule-based modules; trained on the 123-hour CSKnow pro dataset, it was rated **16–59% more humanlike than CS:GO's built-in bot** by 15 human raters, but humans still rated highest and no win rate was reported ([arXiv 2408.13934](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13934v1)). DIAMOND's CS:GO branch (NeurIPS 2024 Spotlight) is a **381M-parameter diffusion world model** trained for 12 days on one RTX 4090 on 87 h of Pearce's scraped data; humans can play Dust2 inside it at ~10 FPS on an RTX 3090, but the authors explicitly trained no agent in it and left "quantitative measurements of the capabilities of the CS:GO world model" to future work ([diamond-wm.github.io](https://diamond-wm.github.io); [arXiv 2405.12399](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.12399)). A 2024 student project built a DDPG agent for 1v1 post-plant CS:GO using YOLOv5 detection and Game State Integration, and **won 39 of 100 rounds against a built-in bot** — losing to it ([beanbois.github.io/CSGO_AI](https://beanbois.github.io/CSGO_AI/)).

The 2025–2026 academic record contains no paper in which an agent plays CS2 end-to-end. DECOY (Winter Simulation Conference 2025) is a discretised waypoint simulator for strategic planning that does not model aiming ([arXiv 2509.06355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06355)); X-Ego-CS, EgoCS-400K, CounterStrike-1K and the Esports World Model Dataset are datasets with no agent trained ([arXiv 2510.19150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19150); [arXiv 2606.18180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18180); [NeurIPS 2026 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/139606); [arXiv 2610.16166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16166)). The hobbyist space is thinner still: the only pixels-in CS2 "player" on GitHub is a five-star YOLO-plus-waypoint deathmatch bot with no reported K/D ([MrM0o0/cs2-deathmatch-bot](https://github.com/MrM0o0/cs2-deathmatch-bot)), and the rest are triggerbots. The one loud claim — Elon Musk's 10 October 2026 post that Tesla/xAI's "Digital Optimus" has Counter-Strike skill that "is good too" — came with no video, metrics, opponent level, or follow-up in the month since ([Drive Tesla Canada](https://driveteslacanada.ca/news/tesla-digital-optimus-ai-diablo-counter-strike-progress/); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/tesla-digital-optimus-gaming-progress/)). Valve's own bots make a weak yardstick: the medium bot scored 1.00 K/D against itself in Pearce's runs, the last documented AI change to them was the 2019 behaviour-tree update, and CS2 release notes since 2024 mention only ladder-teleport and bot-kick fixes ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/csgo/updated-csgo-bot-framework-smart-ai-1032663/); [devtrackers.gg](https://devtrackers.gg/cs2/p/67393310-release-notes-for-6-13-2024)). "Matching the medium bot" therefore means casual-deathmatch level, not competitive play.

## Frontier VLMs score near zero when the game clock keeps running

The intuitive 2026 approach — put GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.7 or Gemini 3.1 Pro in the loop with screenshots — fails on every real-time benchmark. VideoGameBench gives models only raw frames and a controls description, and in its real-time protocol the best models (Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Gemini 2.5 Pro) complete **0.48%** of the game set, GPT-4o 0.09%, with **Doom II at 0% for every model even in the paused "Lite" setting**; failure anecdotes include Gemini emptying its ammo into a corpse it believed was alive ([arXiv 2505.18134 v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134v3)). OmniGameArena (June 2026) built twelve UE5 games including a survival shooter and reports a headline leaderboard in which GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.7 and Gemini 3.1 Pro score 0.1–0.7 normalised — but only under "Paused Decision Quality," which freezes the environment during inference. When measured model latency (GPT-5.5 ~16 s per call, others 4–7 s) is charged to the game clock, actions per episode fall from **43 to 8 for GPT-5.5** and 43 to 17 for Opus 4.6, and the two Opus models collapsed 72–76% on a held-out level variant ([arXiv 2606.09826](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09826v1)). The cleanest head-to-head is a ViZDoom study where a **1.3M-parameter specialist running at 31 ms per decision on CPU scored 178 frags** over ten episodes while GPT-4o-mini (646 ms latency) scored 0, Gemini Flash Lite (920 ms) scored 8, and local Qwen3.5-27B at 13.3 s scored 2 ([arXiv 2604.07385](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07385v1)). The authors of these papers converge on the same diagnosis: stale observations, poor spatial grounding (mislocating enemies), a "knowing-doing gap" between the stated plan and the executed action, and no persistent enemy tracking because each frame is a fresh prompt.

The systems that do achieve real-time pixel-to-keyboard play in commercial 3D games are purpose-trained vision-language-action models, not API calls. ByteDance's Lumine is a 7B Qwen2-VL derivative trained on 1,731 h of Genshin Impact gameplay on 64 H100s; it perceives at **5 Hz and emits 30 Hz actions as six 33 ms chunks**, reaching ~114 ms to first action — but only after a 25× serving optimisation effort on **four H20 GPUs**, and it finished a story act in 56 min against 53 min for expert humans while its authors flag combat against dodging or flying enemies as a weakness ([arXiv 2511.08892](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892v1)). DeepMind's SIMA 2 runs on a Gemini Flash-Lite checkpoint chosen for latency, "approaches human performance" on instruction tasks, and still lists "precise, low-level keyboard and mouse actions" and weak combat "partly due to motor difficulty" as open problems; its control rate is undisclosed ([arXiv 2512.04797](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04797v1); [deepmind.google/sima](https://deepmind.google/sima)). Game-TARS pre-trained on >20k game-hours and beats GPT-5 and Claude 4 Sonnet on ViZDoom, but wrapped its web games in a time-paused sandbox ([arXiv 2510.23691](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23691v1)). On consumer hardware the picture is worse: an unoptimised Qwen2.5-VL-7B on an RTX 4090 needs ~366 ms to first token (~2.7 Hz), ~82 ms with token condensation ([PACE, arXiv 2608.27206](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27206)), and a 9B reactive VLM reached 15 Hz only on a 96 GB RTX Pro 6000 ([arXiv 2606.24470](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24470v1)).

Where real-time FPS play actually works in production, the architecture is a hybrid that keeps the VLM out of the aim loop. Tencent's GIGA, deployed in Peacekeeper Elite and The Finals, pairs a VLM reasoning layer that issues "skill chunks" with an **LSTM RL policy chosen over a Transformer because inference latency matters**, and feeds that policy engine ray-casts rather than pixels ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/25084/tencent-unveils-shooter-ai-agent-that-rejects-player-orders)). PORTAL has a frozen Qwen2.5-32B-Coder write behaviour trees offline so "no LLM inference happens during gameplay" ([arXiv 2503.13356](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13356v1)). GameSense (ACL 2025) lets a VLM author rule- or DQN-based modules and reaches **85% DOOM shooting success versus 10% for Cradle**, which had to pause the game to wait for GPT-4o ([arXiv 2503.21263](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21263v1); [Cradle wiki](https://github.com/BAAI-Agents/Cradle/wiki)). The Latent Bridge quantifies the split — a frozen 8B thinking VLM at ~1.5 s per response injects latent tokens into a 9B fast model at ~33 ms per tick, lifting MsPacman scores 57% — and notes the bridge only helps where deliberate reasoning actually beats reflexes ([arXiv 2606.24470](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24470v1)). For Counter-Strike this implies a VLM is useful, if at all, as a ~0.5–1 Hz strategic layer consuming a minimap and text situation reports and emitting "rotate," "hold angle," or "retake," on top of a sub-50 ms learned policy that does the aiming and moving. No published system has this shape for CS, and no LLM/VLM agent has beaten skilled humans in any competitive real-time game.

## Open behavioural-cloning foundation models now play DOOM and Quake, but not yet Counter-Strike

The fast-policy line advanced the most. The 2025–2026 frontier for pixels-in/keyboard-mouse-out agents is behavioural cloning on thousands of hours of gameplay. Elefant AI's Open Pixels2Play (January 2026) trains 150M–1.2B-parameter decoder-only transformers on **~8,300 h / ~600M image-action pairs across 44 titles including DOOM, Quake and Call of Duty** at 20 FPS on 192×192 inputs, emitting eight action tokens per step (four keys, mouse dx/dy as quantile bins refined by truncated-normal sampling, two mouse buttons); it fits a data scaling law L(D)=1.111+(17/D)^0.2336 that is still falling at full data, uses <1% DAgger-style correction data, and runs at **80/64/62/40 FPS on an RTX 5090** for the 150M/300M/600M/1.2B models against a 20 Hz target; code, data and checkpoints are released ([arXiv 2601.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04575v2); [Player2 blog](https://blog.player2.game/p/scaling-behavior-cloning-improves)). NVIDIA's NitroGen (CVPR 2026 oral) scales to **~40,000 h across >1,000 games** by parsing on-screen gamepad overlays, uses a SigLIP 2 encoder and a 500M flow-matching diffusion transformer emitting 16-action chunks, and shows 10–52% relative improvement when fine-tuned on held-out games — but its action space is **gamepad only**, keyboard-mouse titles are underrepresented, and it omits inference Hz ([arXiv 2601.02427](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427v1); [HF nvidia/NitroGen](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NitroGen)). Cortex (July 2026) shows the low-budget end: a frozen **DINOv3** encoder plus a six-layer transformer with 10.98M trainable parameters, trained on the 475 h Quake subset of P2P, where one sampled epoch takes **3.3 minutes on an RTX 5080**; 19 of 20 Quake E1M1 episodes record a kill but none finishes the level, and the author attributes residual failures to covariate shift ([arXiv 2607.22739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22739)).

Two caveats temper the enthusiasm. First, none of these models reports win rates against humans in any shooter; Pixels2Play's "human-level competence" in DOOM is a qualitative blog claim, and human raters merely preferred larger models' clips ([arXiv 2601.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04575v2)). Second, zero-shot transfer is poor: on OmniGameArena's UE5 games both Open-P2P and NitroGen "collapse to near zero on all but a handful," with Open-P2P scoring 0.000 on the survival shooter ([arXiv 2606.09826](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09826v1)). These are initialisations, not CS2 players; fine-tuning on in-domain data is mandatory, consistent with NitroGen's own finding that even 30 h of target-game data yields up to 52% relative gains on combat tasks.

Action parameterisation is unsettled, and this matters for aiming. VPT used discretised mouse at 20 Hz; Dreamer 4 a 121-class foveated mouse; Open P2P quantile bins; NitroGen continuous chunks via flow matching; Riot/modl.ai a single 165-way yaw×pitch choice; Tencent separate 13-way yaw and 8-way pitch heads ([arXiv 2206.11795](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.11795); [arXiv 2509.24527](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527v1); [arXiv 2501.00078](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00078v1); [arXiv 2410.04936](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04936v1)). No paper compares them for mouse-aim precision, and every published decision rate (16–20 Hz) is an order of magnitude below the 125–1000 Hz of a competitive mouse; whether sub-50 ms control is needed for human-grade CS aim from pixels is an open experimental question. Recoil control is deterministic per weapon in CS and is either ignored (implicitly averaged by 16 Hz BC) or hard-coded in every published system; a learned pixel policy would need either higher Hz or chunked outputs to express spray control.

World models and RL remain the unproven upgrade path. DreamerV3 (Nature 2025) found Minecraft diamonds from sparse reward, and Dreamer 4 (September 2025) is the only system that trained a policy purely inside a learned world model of a 3D keyboard-mouse game and deployed it to the real game — a ~2B model trained on 2,541 h of VPT data on 256–1024 TPU-v5p, reaching **29% iron-pickaxe and 0.7% diamond success** in real Minecraft ([arXiv 2509.24527](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527v1)). For FPS footage the world models exist (DIAMOND CS:GO, GameNGen DOOM at 20 FPS, SCOPE cross-FPS, Microsoft's WHAM/WHAMM) but none trained a policy inside and transferred it; Genie 3 holds consistency for "a few minutes" with ~1 minute of visual memory, and WHAM's context is ~10 s ([arXiv 2408.14837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14837); [arXiv 2605.23345](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23345); [DeepMind Genie 3](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/); [HF microsoft/wham](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/wham)). Direct RL in the real game is blocked by arithmetic: VPT's RL fine-tune consumed ~1.4×10^10 frames, which at 20 Hz is roughly 22 years of real-time play, feasible only because Minecraft runs headless and parallel ([arXiv 2206.11795](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.11795)); Tencent's deployed Arena Breakout bot used 8 GPUs, 3,200 CPU cores and 6,000 game clients per session with engine access ([arXiv 2410.04936](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04936v1)); and Pearce noted that with no CS API "algorithms must train and run in real-time" ([arXiv 2104.04258](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.04258)). NitroGen's trick of freezing the game's system clock while the model predicts makes a Windows game step-able for evaluation but provides no parallelism ([arXiv 2601.02427](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427v1)). The only superhuman pixel-based FPS result remains DeepMind's FTW on Quake III CTF, which needed ~450,000 games of population-based RL in an instrumented engine ([DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/capture-the-flag-the-emergence-of-complex-cooperative-agents/)).

## CS2 datasets grew to terabytes while Valve pulled one down

Data is where 2026 differs most from 2021. Every new CS2 set uses the same reproducible recipe — replay HLTV pro demos in the real CS2 client, record each player's POV, and parse per-tick inputs with demoparser2 — and every one carries a rights caveat.

| Dataset | Game / content | Scale | Labels | Status (Nov 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pearce & Zhu ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/TeaPearce/CounterStrike_Deathmatch)) | CS:GO Dust2 deathmatch, scraped + expert | 95 h scraped (5.5M frames, ~700 GB) + ~4 h expert | keys, clicks, mouse bins, health/ammo, kill/death flags | Live; non-commercial; HF viewer broken |
| CSKnow / MLMove ([project page](https://davidbdurst.com/mlmove/)) | CS:GO pro Retakes, state traces (no pixels) | 123 h at 16 Hz, 17K rounds | positions, inferred movement | Live; requester-pays S3 (30 GB) |
| OpenCS2 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/blanchon/opencs2_dataset)) | CS2 pro demos, POV video | 165,270 POV rounds, 2,974 h, **10.6 TB**, 720p/32 fps | 64 Hz ticks: keys, mouse delta, view angles, fire, position, health | Live; renders CC-BY-4.0, .dem "bound by tournament terms" |
| Reka CS2-10k ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/RekaAI/CS2-10k)) | CS2 pro demos, POV video | 600K+ clips, 10,000+ h, 720p/48 fps | per-frame actions, mouse deltas, pose | **Removed after Valve takedown notice** |
| EgoCS-400K ([arXiv 2606.18180](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18180)) | CS:GO + CS2, 13 maps, 10 POVs/round | 400K+ videos, 10K+ h | view-angle deltas, buttons via demoparser2 | "Data coming soon"; no license stated |
| Esports World Model Datasets ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ml-resources/Esports_world_model_datasets)) | CS2 slice (plus SC2, Dota 2) | 50 matches, 138 h POV footage, 16 fps | player_inputs / player_states parquet | Live; CC BY-NC 4.0 |

OpenCS2 is currently the only large downloadable CS2 pixels-plus-inputs set, and the CS2-10k takedown — the Hugging Face card now reads that the dataset "is no longer available due to a takedown notice from Valve" — is a concrete precedent that rendered-demo datasets can vanish, so anyone building on OpenCS2 should mirror it locally ([HF RekaAI/CS2-10k](https://huggingface.co/datasets/RekaAI/CS2-10k)). Two domain caveats apply to all of them: the footage is competitive pro rounds, not the deathmatch mode in which a first agent is easiest to evaluate, and the "mouse" labels are per-tick view-angle deltas derived from demos rather than hardware counts, so a policy trained on them learns angle-delta targets that must be converted to mouse counts at a known sensitivity ([EgoCS-400K page](https://egocs-400k.github.io/)). Because the renderers are open — Reka's `cs2-dem-renderer` (MIT, Linux-first, demoinfocs-golang parse, CS2 launched via Steam with a server plugin executing a JSON action sequence, TGA frames piped to ffmpeg at 48 fps) and CS Demo Manager — generating a fresh dataset from newly downloaded HLTV demos is feasible and arguably safer than redistributing someone else's ([GitHub reka-ai/cs2-dem-renderer](https://github.com/reka-ai/cs2-dem-renderer); [CSDM video docs](https://cs-demo-manager.com/docs/guides/video)). Expect breakage: demoinfocs-golang shipped fixes for CS2 updates in January, April, July and September 2026, and the renderer plugin "breaks on game updates" ([demoinfocs-golang releases](https://github.com/markus-wa/demoinfocs-golang/releases)).

The parsing side is mature. demoparser2 exposes per-tick `pitch`, `yaw`, velocity, the button bitmask with derived FORWARD/LEFT/FIRE booleans, and raw usercmd fields including `usercmd_mouse_dx/dy` and subtick moves, parsing 50 demos (4.6 GB) in 6.14 s ([GitHub LaihoE/demoparser](https://github.com/LaihoE/demoparser)); awpy 3.0 (Rust core, Polars) and demoinfocs-golang v6 with full usercmd decoding are alternatives ([awpy changelog](https://awpy.readthedocs.io/en/stable/changelog.html)). Whether `usercmd_mouse_dx/dy` are true hardware deltas or re-derived is undocumented.

Running CS2 offline is well supported but has no ready-made RL environment. A dedicated server installs via SteamCMD (app 730, anonymous login, ~60 GB), needs no GSLT on LAN, accepts `-insecure` to disable VAC, and is containerised as `joedwards32/cs2` with env vars for cheats, bot difficulty (0–3), bot quota and game mode ([Valve wiki (DE)](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/De/Counter-Strike_2/Dedicated_Servers); [joedwards32/CS2](https://github.com/joedwards32/CS2)). Valve's Game State Integration posts JSON with player health, armor, `round_kills`, `round_totaldmg`, weapon state, `match_stats`, round phase and the local player's position/forward vector, but it is event-driven with ~0.1 s throttle/buffer floors, so it serves as a reward and episode-boundary channel, not a 60 Hz observation; dense rewards need a CounterStrikeSharp server plugin or server log parsing ([cs2-gsi README](https://docs.rs/crate/cs2-gsi/latest/source/README.md)). `host_timescale` is listed as CS2-compatible under `sv_cheats`, but community reports describe rendering artefacts at non-unity timescale, and no source confirms it keeps a networked client coherent ([TotalCSGO](https://totalcsgo.com/commands/hosttimescale); [r/GlobalOffensive](https://lr.in.psf.lt/r/GlobalOffensive/comments/1e4orkv/recoil_in_cs2_is_still_not_fixed/ldip1wa/?context=3)). CS2 ships only Windows and native-Vulkan Linux builds (macOS support ended October 2023), there is no published recipe for multiple clients per machine or for headless operation, and the CS:GO-era gym-csgo (Xvfb + Proton, last touched December 2023) does not transfer to the native Linux client ([Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/730/CounterStrike_2/); [gym-csgo](https://github.com/iksnagreb/gym-csgo)). The `csgo_legacy` beta branch remains installable in 2026 for offline bots and old tooling, which is the only way to reuse Pearce's models as-is ([Fragster, Mar 2026](https://www.fragster.com/csgo-on-steam-in-2026-how-to-install-the-legacy-build-and-why-comeback-talk-is-trending-again/)).

Input injection is the trap most newcomers hit. **CS2 reads the mouse as raw input with no in-game toggle**, so SendInput/pyautogui-class motion moves the cursor but not the view; practitioners report keys and clicks working but aim resetting to centre, and moving to hardware HID modules ([csdb raw input](https://csdb.gg/does-cs2-have-raw-input/); [V2EX thread](https://www.v2ex.com/t/1044983)). Working routes are the Windows Interception kernel driver via pyinterception, Linux `uinput` virtual devices via python-evdev or ydotool, or a microcontroller HID (Arduino Leonardo, RP2040, CH9329) that is indistinguishable from a real mouse ([pyinterception](https://github.com/kennyhml/pyinterception); [python-evdev](https://python-evdev.readthedocs.io/); [pico-hid](https://pypi.org/project/pico-hid)). Pearce's CS:GO agent required raw input off, which CS2 no longer allows. Screen capture is a solved problem at the 20–30 Hz a policy needs: DXGI Desktop Duplication via dxcam/bettercam delivers ~130–160 fps of fresh 1080p frames on Windows, and X11 XShm is the fast path on Linux (Wayland screenshots fall to ~2 fps) ([rustcam benchmark](https://pypi.org/project/rustcam); [Lemmy thread](https://lemmy.johnnei.org/post/669182/1411642)). Hardware is modest: CS2's floor is four CPU threads, 8 GB RAM and a 1 GB DX11 or Kepler+/GCN+ Vulkan GPU, Pearce's CNN ran at 16 Hz on an average gaming GPU, DIAMOND's far heavier model runs at 10 FPS on an RTX 3090, so an RTX 3060-class card hosts a low-res client plus a CNN/transformer policy at 20–30 Hz, with disk (~85 GB client + ~60 GB server) the surprise ([Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/730/CounterStrike_2/); [DIAMOND csgo README](https://github.com/eloialonso/diamond/blob/csgo/README.md)). Nobody has published the glue; every component of a CS2 environment exists separately.

## A fair "beats humans" test needs a 250 ms delay and a pro-vetted rulebook

"Can an AI beat humans at Counter-Strike" is underspecified until opponents, handicaps and metrics are fixed, and the precedents are unambiguous that unconstrained agents win on reflexes rather than skill. In DeepMind's Quake III CTF tournament, humans took **559 ms from first sighting to a tag attempt versus 258 ms for FTW agents**, and human tagging accuracy was 48% against the agents' 80%; DeepMind imposed a **267 ms observation delay** and artificially reduced aim accuracy, and strong humans still won only 21% of games ([arXiv 1807.01281](https://arxiv.org/html/1807.01281v1); [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/capture-the-flag-the-emergence-of-complex-cooperative-agents/)). OpenAI Five acted every fourth frame for an effective **217 ms reaction** after raising it from 80 ms to be "much closer to human level" ([arXiv 1912.06680](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.06680v1)). AlphaStar's December 2018 version drew sustained criticism for a global camera and "surgical precision" clicks; the Battle.net version that reached Grandmaster used a camera interface and a cap of **22 agent actions per 5 seconds** developed with and approved by a professional player ([DeepMind AlphaStar blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/alphastar-grandmaster-level-in-starcraft-ii-using-multi-agent-reinforcement-learning/); [Wikipedia: AlphaStar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AlphaStar_(software))). Musk's proposed Grok 5 versus T1 League of Legends match promised camera-only vision and human-level reaction and click limits, a sign that the public now treats these constraints as table stakes ([sheepesports](https://www.sheepesports.com/articles/elon-musk-wants-grok-5-to-compete-versus-the-best-human-team-of-league-of-legends/en)). Pearce & Zhu imposed no delay or mouse-speed cap and should be read as a humanlike-imitation result, not a fair-comparison result.

Human baselines give concrete numbers to anchor those handicaps. Population simple visual reaction time has a **median of ~251 ms** (HumanBenchmark, >15M clicks), and a University of Sheffield study of >300 Counter-Strike players found experts (>5,000 h) only **~90 ms faster** than novices on lab visual-processing tasks, with no multitasking advantage ([IB Maths Resources](https://ibmathsresources.com/2015/03/20/reaction-times-how-fast-are-you); [University of Sheffield](https://sheffield.ac.uk/news/are-counter-strike-players-better-decision-making-study-reveals-faster-brain-processing-expert)). In-game sighting-to-damage latency is far longer: Leetify's per-rank "Time to Damage" averages sit around **550–640 ms** from Silver through Global Elite profiles, matching the Quake CTF human figure ([Leetify @hilex](https://leetify.com/@hilex); [Leetify @dope](https://leetify.com/@dope)). Pro mouse settings define a human-plausible kinematic envelope: across 1,007 pros the **median eDPI is 800 (mean 849)**, which at 400 DPI implies in-game sensitivity ~2.0 and roughly 25–45 cm per 360° turn ([CSDB pro sensitivity](https://csdb.gg/data/pro-sensitivity/); [arXiv 2203.12050](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12050)). Opponent skill should be stated in Premier rating terms: the January 2026 distribution has a **median around 11,000, top 10% starting near 17,400**, and only 0.01% above 30,000 ([EsportsTales](https://www.esportstales.com/csgo/rank-distribution-and-percentage-of-players); [MetaBot](https://metabot.gg/en/cs2/ranks)). The "pros react in 150–200 ms" figures that circulate online are unsourced, and no vendor publishes HS% or K/D tables by rank, so those anchors must be sampled from profiles.

The synthesis of prior work yields a minimum constraint set for a credible pixels-in CS comparison: screen-only observation at a normal FOV and resolution; an added observation-to-action delay of roughly 250–270 ms; an action-rate cap (16–20 Hz is what every published agent uses); a mouse-speed or aim-precision handicap bounded by the pro eDPI and cm/360 envelope; and constraints reviewed by an experienced player before the study. For protocol, FTW's design is the most reusable template — fixed-duration games, 40 humans across five tournaments, Elo extended to teams, humans pooled into top-20% "strong" and 80% "average" tiers because of high per-player variance — combined with Pearce's CS-native metrics of kills per minute and kills per death over fixed-length deathmatch ([arXiv 1807.01281](https://arxiv.org/html/1807.01281v1)). None of the FPS studies reports confidence intervals or power analyses, so pre-registering sample size and reporting CIs on kill-rate differences would by itself improve on the state of the art. Humanlikeness is a separate axis: the modl.ai/Riot study had 20 judges rate 32 fifteen-second clips and found **30% of bot clips judged human versus 60% of human clips**, with no significance tests; MLMove used TrueSkill rankings from 15 judges; the AAMAS 2025 survey recommends third-person judging and reports a CNN+RNN bot detector at F1 0.92; BotPrize 2026 weights first- and third-person judging 50/50 ([arXiv 2501.00078](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00078v1); [arXiv 2505.20011](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20011v3); [BotPrize 2026](https://botprize2026.ai2.upv.es/)).

The legitimacy constraints are the hardest and must shape the design from day one. The Steam Subscriber Agreement §4.C states "You may not use any form of scripts, bots, macros, or other non-human-controlled systems," with no research exemption, and VAC bans are permanent ([Steam Subscriber Agreement](https://store.steampowered.com/agreement)). CS2 runs in Trusted Mode by default, where any injection is "subject to a VAC ban," and the Valve Developer Wiki says unsigned injected code requires `-insecure` mode, which bars VAC-secured servers ([Steam Support: Trusted Mode](https://help.steampowered.com/faqs/view/09A0-4879-4353-EF95)). Behavioural detection now targets synthetic input itself: VACnet has run since 2018 with 80–95% conviction rates on submitted cases, VAC Live adds "real-time behavioral analysis," a July 2026 patch closed a subtick-input exploit that starved VACnet of data, and FACEIT's "Human Input Detection" went live on 5 August 2026 to catch cheaters "by their in-game actions, not by the software they use" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/vacnet-csgo); [SteamDB](https://steamdb.com/en/news/cs2-anti-cheat-update-vac-live); [ExitLag](https://www.exitlag.com/blog/vacnet-update-cs2/); [esportnow.gg](https://esportnow.gg/cs2/news/new-faceit-anti-cheat-how-does-human-input-detection-work)). Valve has reversed false-positive ban waves in December 2025 and January 2026, and has published no statement on AI research agents ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/counter-strike-2/vac-ban-wave-valve-january-2026)). The defensible configuration, and the one every prior project adopted, is a dedicated throwaway Steam account with no inventory, a LAN-only dedicated server launched with `-insecure`, OS-level capture and OS-level or hardware HID input (never process injection), and never connecting to matchmaking, VAC-secured or FACEIT servers; Pearce's README already warned that "Valve may detect the usage of some of these scripts in game" ([GitHub TeaPearce](https://github.com/TeaPearce/Counter-Strike_Behavioural_Cloning); [gym-csgo](https://github.com/iksnagreb/gym-csgo)). Human opponents for the evaluation therefore have to join that LAN server too, which is a feature: it is the only way to control opponent skill and record ground truth.

## The best-supported build fine-tunes an open BC model on CS2 Dust2 deathmatch

Weighing the evidence, the options for a pixels-in/keyboard-mouse-out CS2 agent on a single consumer GPU rank as follows.

| Approach | Evidence for | Evidence against | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frontier VLM in the loop (GPT-5.5 / Claude / Gemini) | Strong on paused/turn-based games | 0% Doom II; 4–16 s per decision; actions cut 50–80% when latency is charged | Not viable for aim/movement; usable only as a ~1 Hz planner |
| Local 7B VLA from scratch (Lumine-style) | 30 Hz chunked actions, real-time in Genshin | 64 H100 training, 4×H20 inference; combat is its stated weakness | Out of budget; motor skill unproven |
| Fine-tune Open-P2P 300M–600M | Native keyboard-mouse at 20 Hz; trained on DOOM/Quake/CoD; 62–64 FPS on RTX 5090; open data/code/weights; scaling law still improving | Zero-shot collapse on new games; no human win rates; DOOM claims qualitative | **Best-supported initialisation** |
| Frozen DINOv3/SigLIP + small head (Cortex-style) | 11M trainable params, 3.3 min/epoch on RTX 5080; cheapest credible 2026 recipe | Never finished a Quake level; covariate shift dominant | Best fallback on a single mid-range GPU |
| Pearce-style CNN+LSTM from scratch | Only peer-reviewed CS result; 16 Hz on average GPU | 4× Titan X × days per model; medium-bot ceiling; CS:GO visuals | Reproduce as baseline via `csgo_legacy`, not as the main model |
| NitroGen fine-tune | 40k h pretraining, +10–52% transfer | Gamepad-only action space | Poor fit for keyboard-mouse CS |
| RL inside DIAMOND-style CS world model | 381M model trains in 12 days on one 4090; Dreamer 4 closed the loop in Minecraft | No FPS policy ever trained in a world model; combat/hit-registration fidelity unknown; Dreamer 4 used 256–1024 TPUs | Phase-2 research bet, not the first build |

The recommended model choice is therefore Open Pixels2Play at 300M or 600M parameters as the initialisation — the only open checkpoint that is keyboard-mouse native, FPS-pretrained, real-time on a consumer GPU, and shipped with a training recipe and dataset — with the Cortex pattern (frozen DINOv3, small transformer head) as the budget alternative when the GPU is an RTX 3060/4060-class card rather than a 5080/5090 ([arXiv 2601.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04575v2); [arXiv 2607.22739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22739)). Keep the P2P action parameterisation (eight discrete tokens per 20 Hz step with quantile-binned mouse deltas), but reserve the right to add a short action chunk (Lumine's six 33 ms sub-steps or NitroGen's chunking) once the baseline works, since that is the only published mechanism that could express CS recoil control without a hand-coded module.

Data should come in two layers. For pretraining breadth, OpenCS2's 2,974 POV-hours with 64 Hz keys and mouse deltas is the obvious in-domain corpus, converted from view-angle deltas to mouse counts at a fixed sensitivity and mirrored locally against takedown risk ([HF blanchon/opencs2_dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/blanchon/opencs2_dataset)). For the actual target distribution — Dust2 deathmatch against bots and humans on a LAN server — record your own play with synchronised screen capture and raw input logging at 20 Hz plus GSI health/kill labels, exactly as the Agentic-CS2 hobby project does at ~15 FPS on an RTX 4050 ([GitHub kvsznmate/Agentic-CS2](https://github.com/kvsznmate/Agentic-CS2)). Pearce reached medium-bot level with 95 h of scraped data plus ~4 h of clean expert play; the P2P scaling law says loss keeps falling with more data, so a few hundred hours of in-domain deathmatch is the realistic target, and the 1–5% DAgger-style correction data that P2P and Cortex both identify as the cure for covariate shift should be budgeted from the start. If the recruitment of strong human demonstrators is hard, the same renderer pipeline that built OpenCS2 can turn freshly downloaded HLTV demos into additional labelled POV footage.

The environment is assembled from parts that all exist: the `joedwards32/cs2` dedicated-server container with `-insecure`, `sv_cheats 1`, pinned `bot_difficulty` and `bot_quota`; a Linux client (native Vulkan, X11 session for XShm capture, `uinput` for raw-input-accepted mouse and keys) or a Windows client with dxcam and the Interception driver; GSI for rewards and episode boundaries, upgraded to a CounterStrikeSharp plugin if dense per-frame reward is needed; and a throwaway account that never touches Valve or FACEIT servers. An RTX 3060-class GPU suffices for the client at low resolution plus a 300M policy at 20 Hz; training a 600M model on a few hundred hours is a multi-day single-GPU job on a 4090/5090-class card, and a DIAMOND-scale world model is 12 days on a 4090 if that phase is attempted.

The evaluation should be designed before training, in three tiers. Tier one reproduces Pearce's protocol — kills per minute and kills per death over fixed-length Dust2 deathmatch against easy, medium and expert bots — to establish whether the agent clears the only published bar. Tier two adds the fairness handicaps (≈250 ms added observation delay, 16–20 Hz action cap, mouse-speed envelope inside the pro eDPI/cm-per-360 range, screen-only observation at normal FOV), has a strong player review them, and then runs within-subjects deathmatch sessions where the same human cohort faces both the agent and a human of known Premier tier, pooled into tiers FTW-style, with pre-registered sample size and confidence intervals. Tier three runs a third-person clip Turing test with at least 50 judges and binomial CIs, fixing the statistics missing from the Riot/modl.ai study. The stated hypothesis "an AI can beat humans at Counter-Strike" is then tested as "under pro-vetted human-like constraints, the agent's K/D against players at Premier tier X exceeds 1.0 with CI excluding 1.0," which is falsifiable and comparable to prior work.

Expectations should be calibrated to the record. Pure behavioural cloning from pixels has topped out at casual-human deathmatch level in the one measured CS case, and the 2026 multi-game models — despite 80× more data — have not demonstrated a pixel-BC agent beating a competent human in any deathmatch shooter. Beating skilled humans requires skill beyond imitation, which means RL, and both available routes are undemonstrated for FPS: imagination RL inside a learned CS world model (DIAMOND shows the model fits on one 4090; Dreamer 4 shows the loop works in Minecraft; nobody has shown a world model that renders hit registration faithfully enough to learn aim in) or a Tencent-style farm of parallel clients, for which CS2 offers no multi-instance or headless recipe. A VLM planner at ~1 Hz is cheap to add on top and is the pattern production systems use, but the Latent Bridge result warns it only helps where deliberate reasoning beats reflexes, which in a 1v1 deathmatch it mostly does not.

## Conclusion

The striking fact of 2024–2026 is that the Counter-Strike research community has routed around the agent problem rather than through it. Terabytes of rendered pro CS2 footage, open demo parsers that recover sub-tick mouse deltas, a playable neural Dust2 and multi-game BC checkpoints are all in place, yet no one has connected them into a CS2 player, and the one party with every ingredient (Valve) has signalled through a takedown and an escalating behavioural anti-cheat program that it treats synthetic input as hostile by default. The absence of a 2025–2026 CS2 agent paper is less a sign that the problem is uninteresting than that the engineering gate — synchronised 20 Hz capture-and-inject on a game with mandatory raw input and no API, run entirely on a LAN — is where projects die before any learning happens. Anyone who builds that gate cleanly and publishes it as a Gymnasium-style environment will have made the field's most useful contribution, independent of how well the agent plays.

The educational framing also changes what counts as a win. The 2021 ceiling was set with 95 hours and a CNN; the 2026 toolkit offers FPS-pretrained transformers, pretrained vision encoders, and thirty times more in-domain data, so merely re-establishing the Pearce numbers in CS2 and then measuring how K/D moves with data, action-chunk length and decision rate would answer open questions nobody has published on — above all whether sub-50 ms mouse control from pixels is required for human-grade aim. Framing the project as "can a fairly handicapped pixel agent reach Premier-median K/D in deathmatch" is the version of "can AI beat humans at Counter-Strike" that the evidence says is both testable on one GPU and genuinely unresolved.
